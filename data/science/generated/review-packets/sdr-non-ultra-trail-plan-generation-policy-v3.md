### Linked evidence

#### `trail-resource.strength-support-not-short-block-promise` — moderate

Resistance training can improve muscle function in healthy adults, including inexperienced trainees. Some runner strength protocols improve running performance, but these findings do not validate a fourteen-day trail plan or guarantee a personal performance gain.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `currier-2026-resistance-position-stand`, `llanos-lagos-2024`
- **Limitations:** ACSM included interventions lasting 6–52 weeks, predominantly inexperienced trainees; runner review interventions lasted 6–40 weeks.; Runner review certainty ranged from very low to moderate; high-load and combined effects do not establish efficacy of this low-load introductory template.; The runner review included participants over 16; Praxys remains adult-only.

#### `trail-resource.introductory-dose-is-a-guardrail` — moderate

A low-volume introduction and avoiding repetition failure are evidence-consistent options. Published studies and guidance permit multiple useful prescriptions; they do not establish exact personal starting sets, repetitions, load, rest or repetitions-in-reserve thresholds.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `currier-2026-resistance-position-stand`, `blagrove-2018-running-strength`, `stohanzl-2018-minimal-strength`
- **Limitations:** ACSM describes benefits with a single set and greater gains with additional sets; its primary recommendation is at least twice weekly, not proof of one optimum.; Blagrove reports studies often starting at 1–2 sets, typical 2–3 minute rests, and predominantly 2–3 weekly sessions. These are study descriptions/reviewer recommendations.; The 41-woman 10-week trial compared one or two 30-minute strength sessions with running-only while reallocating a fixed three-hour weekly budget; abstract reports improvement within concurrent groups, not proof of superiority or a fourteen-day effect.; ACSM discusses 2–3 RIR but explicitly notes insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR targets; Praxys targets remain guardrails.

#### `trail-resource.strength-does-not-guarantee-injury-prevention` — moderate

One self-directed strength programme in first-time marathon runners did not significantly reduce overuse injuries causing noncompletion or improve mean finishing time; strength inclusion cannot be presented as an injury-prevention guarantee.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `toresdahl-2020`
- **Limitations:** One 12-week, three-times-weekly programme; observation participants could also perform strength training.; Self-reported adherence/injury and no direct technique observation limit interpretation; this does not establish that all strength training lacks benefit.

#### `trail-resource.treadmill-is-context-not-terrain-equivalence` — moderate

Motorized treadmill running provides a running training context with many biomechanical similarities to overground running, alongside meaningful differences. It does not establish equivalent outdoor trail ascent, descent, footing or performance exposure.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `van-hooren-2020-treadmill-biomechanics`, `miller-2019-treadmill-physiology`
- **Limitations:** Biomechanics review covered non-inclined, quasi-constant-speed motorized running, not technical trails; outcome certainty was mostly low/very low.; Physiological/perceptual differences can depend on speed; no universal 1% incline correction is established for this policy.; These are acute comparisons, not longitudinal evidence for replacing outdoor trail training.

#### `trail-resource.uphill-downhill-specific-and-dose-uncertain` — low

Uphill and downhill running impose different mechanical and physiological demands. Uphill training is a plausible optional module, but heterogeneous training evidence does not choose a universal gradient, session dose or superiority over conventional training.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `lu-2025-graded-running`, `tham-2026-uphill-training`
- **Limitations:** Lu is a narrative synthesis; inaccessible and non-English evidence was excluded and no formal risk-of-bias synthesis is reported.; Tham included 13 small trials, predominantly athletes/males; only seven entered quantitative analyses and pooled outcomes did not show significant superiority for strength, jumping or aerobic capacity.; Many interventions were high-intensity sprints at substantially steeper grades, not the proposed easy 2% familiarization template.; The proposed gradient, duration and frequency are Praxys guardrails, not validated safe or effective individual doses.

#### `trail-resource.downhill-adaptation-not-clearance` — low

Repeated downhill exposure may attenuate soreness in source-specific protocols, but soreness and neuromuscular fatigue can adapt differently. Running fitness, strength work or absence of soreness cannot establish descent readiness or an initial safe downhill dose.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `bontemps-2025-downhill-fatigue`, `tallis-2024-downhill-female`
- **Limitations:** Small supervised laboratory studies with specific grades, speeds and prior familiarization.; Bontemps found reduced quadriceps soreness after ten sessions without attenuation of neuromuscular fatigue to a standardized bout; Tallis did not measure neuromuscular recovery directly.; Neither study validates outdoor descent dose, a 42/56-day readiness cutoff, a monthly exposure budget or equivalence with gym training.

#### `trail-resource.concurrent-scheduling-requires-context` — low

Running and strength can be combined within a programme. Acute fatigue and available time should be considered, but the reviewed evidence does not establish a universal recovery interval or a minutes-to-minutes physiological load equivalence.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `blagrove-2018-running-strength`, `currier-2026-resistance-position-stand`, `stohanzl-2018-minimal-strength`
- **Limitations:** Blagrove discusses separate days, more than three hours within-day, and at least 24 hours before intensive running as practical recommendations, not individual safety laws.; A fixed available-time budget is a scheduling constraint; reallocating time does not prove equal physiological exposure.; The current single-session-per-day candidate limit is a software scope decision, not a biological rule.

#### `trail-resource.subjective-effort-is-not-a-universal-zone` — low

Comfortable speech and perceived effort can support practical aerobic intensity adjustment, but exact subjective thresholds and strength RIR values cannot be treated as universal physiological or safety thresholds.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `bok-2022-subjective-intensity`, `currier-2026-resistance-position-stand`
- **Limitations:** Talk-test review was verified at indexed abstract level only; no individual threshold or test equivalence is asserted.; Speed, gradient, symptoms, form and mode remain relevant; comfortable speech does not measure downhill mechanical load.

#### `trail-resource.resource-frequency-does-not-prove-capacity` — low

Resource availability and training exposure are different constructs. Sparse records or zero calendar-week aggregates cannot establish zero biological capacity, and resource calendars do not themselves prove comparable terrain exposure.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-training-resource-adaptation-v1`
- **Sources:** `lu-2025-graded-running`, `bontemps-2025-downhill-fatigue`, `van-hooren-2020-treadmill-biomechanics`
- **Limitations:** No reviewed source prospectively validates the proposed resource presets, exposure-count thresholds, calendar budgets or automated plan generator.; Unknown, no recorded exposure, athlete statements and measured exposure must retain their distinct provenance.

#### `trail-ontology.course-demand-is-multidimensional` — moderate

Trail-running performance and exposure are course-specific and multifactorial. Distance alone does not preserve elevation, grade, surface, technicality, altitude, environment, event format, or support.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-trail-running-goal-ontology-v1`
- **Sources:** `scheer-2020-off-road-definition`, `de-waal-2021-performance-review`, `pastor-2022-distance-determinants`, `scheer-2019-threshold-prediction`
- **Limitations:** The literature does not provide one validated machine-readable schema.; Performance associations do not establish individual plan dose.

#### `non-ultra-trail.course-specific-policy-required` — moderate

Trail performance and training demand are course-specific and multifactorial. A policy must match an explicit course-demand vector and comparable exposure rather than distance, level pace, or one physiological marker alone.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-non-ultra-trail-plan-generation-policy-v1`
- **Sources:** `de-waal-2021-performance-review`, `pastor-2022-distance-determinants`, `bjorklund-2019-short-trail`
- **Limitations:** Associations and prediction models do not establish causal training dose.; The reviewed courses and populations are heterogeneous.

#### `eligibility.recent-history-anchor-without-universal-threshold` — moderate

Abrupt weekly or single-session distance increases are associated with higher running-related injury rates, while the reviewed evidence is heterogeneous and does not establish one universal safe increase. Recent consistency and recent longest-session history are therefore relevant eligibility dimensions, not validated prescription cutoffs.

- **Evidence Review:** `evidence-plan-generation-eligibility-safety-v1`
- **Sources:** `damsted-2019`, `frandsen-2025`, `correia-2024`
- **Limitations:** These studies do not establish causation or an individual safety threshold.; The weekly association was significant at 21 days but not later follow-up points.; The single-session cohort used self-reported injury outcomes and did not validate an automatic plan rule.; The umbrella review found only critically low or low-quality systematic reviews.

### Rejected alternatives

#### Reuse the accepted v2 approval for new strength, treadmill and low-trail-history prescriptions

V2 explicitly deferred these modules and assumed history-rich terrain access; its decision does not authorize the new scope.

#### Block all training when trail exposure is missing, or silently replace missing specialty with equivalent road/gym dose

A distinct basic mode can provide bounded general running and gym value while retaining uncertainty and unsupported course-specific coverage.

#### Treat missing descent as zero or fix sparse history by taking a positive-weeks-only median

Both erase provenance or exposure frequency and can manufacture unsupported capacity or dose.
