## Question and product purpose

For adults aged 18 years or older whose current training-context pattern shows direct half-marathon capability and stable recent running history, what evidence supports and limits suggestion-only outdoor road half-marathon performance planning with optional target time and date, confirmed event context, durability and long-run inputs, tapering, and fueling practice?

Define the evidence boundary for one history-rich outdoor road half-marathon performance policy without inheriting 5 km or 10 km schedule numbers. Separate direct half-marathon capability and observational training associations from mixed-endurance taper, intensity-distribution, recovery, and sports-nutrition evidence. Identify where Praxys still needs explicit product guardrails for history depth, baseline freshness, long-run exposure, quality spacing, progression, execution windows, target communication, fueling prompts, and post-plan evaluation.

## Scope

## Population

- Adults aged 18 years or older
- Runners with current direct outdoor road half-marathon capability
- Runners with stable recent history sufficient to anchor their own training exposure
- Outdoor road half-marathon performance intent with or without a confirmed target event
- Recreational and amateur cohorts as evidence populations, not permanent user identities

## Intervention or exposure

- Half-marathon performance demands, pacing, and prediction inputs
- Weekly running volume, frequency, and longest-run exposure
- Mostly-low-intensity, threshold, interval, and race-specific training organization
- Long-run, recovery, and quality-session spacing
- Pre-event tapering
- During-run carbohydrate intake and fueling-practice or gut-training exposure
- Same-task field results, field tests, laboratory markers, and outcome comparability

## Comparator

- Lower versus higher observed weekly volume or longest-run exposure
- Polarized, pyramidal, threshold-heavy, or other intensity distributions
- Reduced versus maintained training volume, intensity, or frequency during taper
- Field performance versus laboratory or multivariable prediction
- Practiced versus unpracticed during-exercise fueling
- Rest versus different post-half-marathon recovery-running intensities

## Outcomes

- Half-marathon finish time and pacing decline
- Time-trial or endurance performance
- Prediction error and comparability
- Recovery time course
- Gastrointestinal discomfort and carbohydrate malabsorption
- Applicability, adherence burden, and uncertainty

## Inclusion criteria

- Adult human evidence relevant to outdoor road half-marathon performance planning
- Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, controlled studies, observational half-marathon studies, or authoritative sports-nutrition consensus
- Stable DOI, PMID, or publisher metadata
- Direct half-marathon evidence preferred; broader endurance transfer labelled explicitly

## Exclusion criteria

- Beginner or sparse-history plans used as direct evidence for this policy
- Trail, ultra, multisport, rehabilitation, pregnancy-specific, return-to-sport, or medical prescription
- Coaching plans, vendor material, blogs, or uncited conventions
- Metadata-only or inaccessible sources used for strong effect or dosing claims

## Method limitations

- SPORTDiscus, Embase, and Web of Science were not directly screened in this run
- Much half-marathon-specific evidence is observational and several predictor studies are male-only
- Several intensity-distribution and nutrition sources were verified at abstract level only
- No direct female-specific or masters-specific half-marathon planning trial was identified
- Modern durability constructs have little direct adult half-marathon planning evidence

### Quality appraisal

Claims were appraised for directness to adult outdoor road half-marathon planning, study design, self-reported versus measured training, sample size, sex and age representation, ecological validity, and whether results support individual prescription or only population association. Direct half-marathon training-dose evidence is predominantly observational; mixed-endurance taper and nutrition evidence is more mature but indirect.

## Claims

### `road-half-marathon.task-specific-capability-is-multifactor` — low

Half-marathon performance and pacing reflect multiple interacting training, physiological, anthropometric, biomechanical, age, and sex factors rather than one marker. This supports task-specific capability matching but not a universal durability score or single-marker gate.

- **Sources:** `ogueta-alday-2018`, `gomez-molina-2017`, `nikolaidis-2019-ljubljana`, `cuk-2019-vienna`
- **Population:** Adult half-marathon runners; Mechanistic and prediction evidence primarily from men
- **Domain:** Capability matching; Pacing; Performance prediction
- **Limitations:**
  - Predictor studies do not establish causal training prescriptions
  - Direct durability mechanisms and individual thresholds were not validated
  - Male-heavy samples limit subgroup transfer
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Performance-model explained variance: 90.3 to 96.2 percent (Four male half-marathon prediction models in Gomez-Molina et al. 2017)

### `road-half-marathon.volume-and-long-run-are-associative` — moderate

Higher recent weekly running volume and longer single-run exposure are associated with faster half-marathon finish time and less pace decline, but the observational evidence does not establish a universal weekly dose, frequency, longest-run minimum, or injury-safe prescription.

- **Sources:** `fokkema-2020`
- **Population:** Adult recreational half-marathon entrants
- **Domain:** Weekly volume; Longest-run exposure; Pacing decline
- **Limitations:**
  - Self-reported training exposure
  - Categorical observational comparisons rather than randomized dose response
  - No causal proof of safety, optimality, or progression rate
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Finish-time coefficient for more than 32 km per week: -4.19 (range -6.52 to -1.85) minutes (Adjusted observational half-marathon model in 556 runners)
  - Finish-time coefficient for longest run above 21 km: -3.87 (range -6.31 to -1.44) minutes (Adjusted observational half-marathon model in 556 runners)

### `road-half-marathon.taper-is-indirectly-supported` — moderate

Mixed-endurance evidence supports reducing training volume while maintaining intensity and usually frequency before a key event, with the strongest pooled subgroup signal around 8 to 14 days. The evidence does not validate one half-marathon-specific taper template or personal gain.

- **Sources:** `wang-2023`
- **Population:** Endurance athletes, indirectly including road runners
- **Domain:** Taper; Event preparation
- **Limitations:**
  - Mixed sports and event distances
  - Not a direct adult road half-marathon taper trial set
  - Heterogeneous prior training and protocols
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Time-trial performance after taper: -0.45 (range -0.68 to -0.23) standardized_mean_difference (Mixed-endurance meta-analysis; 95% confidence interval)
  - Performance with 41 to 60 percent volume reduction: -0.77 (range -1.23 to -0.3) standardized_mean_difference (Mixed-endurance taper subgroup; 95% confidence interval)
  - Performance with 8 to 14 day taper: -1.47 (range -2.75 to -0.19) standardized_mean_difference (Mixed-endurance taper-duration subgroup; 95% confidence interval)

### `road-half-marathon.fueling-and-gut-practice-supported` — moderate

Carbohydrate intake during exercise is supported around half-marathon-duration efforts, with small amounts or mouth rinse useful around one hour and 30 to 60 grams per hour supported for longer endurance exercise. Practicing intake can reduce gastrointestinal discomfort and carbohydrate malabsorption, but tolerance and race duration remain individual and gut-training performance effects are uncertain.

- **Sources:** `burke-2011`, `burke-2019-iaaf`, `martinez-2023`, `podlogar-2022`
- **Population:** Adult endurance athletes; Half-marathon runners whose expected duration and tolerance make intake relevant
- **Domain:** During-run fueling; Gastrointestinal tolerance; Fueling practice
- **Limitations:**
  - Most numeric guidance is broader endurance evidence rather than half-marathon-only evidence
  - Expected duration varies substantially within the same race distance
  - No universal carbohydrate-loading rule applies to every half-marathon runner
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Gastrointestinal discomfort reduction: 47.0 percent (Two repeated-carbohydrate-feeding protocols summarized in Martinez et al. 2023)
  - Carbohydrate malabsorption reduction: 45.0 to 54.0 percent (Two gut-training studies summarized in Martinez et al. 2023)

### `road-half-marathon.direct-field-baseline-preferred-with-error` — low

Recent same-task or closely comparable field performance is more direct for half-marathon capability than laboratory markers or multivariable predictions alone. Even validated prediction models retain meaningful error and do not support a personal success probability.

- **Sources:** `alvero-cruz-2019`, `nikolaidis-knechtle-2023-hm-predictors`, `gomez-molina-2017`
- **Population:** Recreational male runners in the direct predictor studies
- **Domain:** Baseline hierarchy; Target communication; Outcome uncertainty
- **Limitations:**
  - Small or male-only samples
  - Field-test equations are not universal replacements for same-task results
  - Prediction error remains material for an individual goal
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Cooper-test half-marathon prediction standard error: 3.78 minutes (23 recreational male runners in Alvero-Cruz et al. 2019)
  - Laboratory-model half-marathon prediction standard error: 5.28 minutes (23 recreational male runners in Alvero-Cruz et al. 2019)
  - Validation standard deviation around prediction bias: 12.8 minutes (134 male recreational runners in Nikolaidis and Knechtle 2023)

### `road-half-marathon.exact-long-run-dose-unproven` — low

Meaningful longest-run exposure is associated with half-marathon performance, but no reviewed randomized evidence establishes an exact longest-run share of weekly volume, exact ceiling, mandatory distance, or universal progression rule.

- **Sources:** `fokkema-2020`
- **Population:** Adult recreational half-marathon entrants
- **Domain:** Long run; Progression
- **Limitations:**
  - One categorical observational half-marathon study anchors the direct evidence
  - A longest run above race distance cannot be generalized as a requirement
  - Injury non-association in the cohort is not a safety guarantee

### `road-half-marathon.intensity-distribution-no-universal-winner` — moderate

Mostly-low-intensity organization with some threshold, interval, or race-specific work is broadly supported, but direct and mixed-distance evidence does not establish one universally superior polarized, pyramidal, threshold-heavy, quality-session count, or race-pace distribution for half-marathon planning. In the cited 10 km trial, both groups improved and the primary between-group difference was not statistically significant.

- **Sources:** `munoz-2014`, `campos-2022`, `rosenblat-2025`, `boullosa-2020`
- **Population:** Recreational and trained endurance runners; Indirect transfer to adult road half-marathon planning
- **Domain:** Intensity distribution; Threshold and interval work
- **Limitations:**
  - Different zone definitions, event distances, and sports
  - No direct adult road half-marathon intervention establishes an exact distribution
  - One to two quality sessions per week remains an indirect coaching-oriented recommendation
  - The cited 5.0 versus 3.6 percent trial changes did not differ significantly in the primary comparison
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Within-group ten-kilometre improvement under polarized training: 5.0 percent (Recreational-runner trial; primary between-group difference was not significant)
  - Within-group ten-kilometre improvement under between-threshold training: 3.6 percent (Recreational-runner trial; primary between-group difference was not significant)

### `road-half-marathon.recovery-spacing-unresolved` — low

Direct evidence does not establish one optimal recovery strategy or exact spacing rule between half-marathon training sessions. Post-race studies suggest different outcomes may recover over roughly one to three days and that higher-intensity recovery running can delay recovery, but these findings do not define a universal schedule rule.

- **Sources:** `li-2024`, `wang-2025-hm-recovery`, `zhou-2026-hm-mri-recovery`
- **Population:** Adult endurance athletes; Recreational runners after simulated or completed half-marathon efforts
- **Domain:** Recovery; Quality-session spacing; Post-event behavior
- **Limitations:**
  - Small direct half-marathon samples
  - Outcomes differ across biomechanics, proprioception, and MRI markers
  - Post-race recovery does not directly prescribe routine training-session spacing

### `road-half-marathon.subgroup-dose-rules-unproven` — low

Women and older adults are represented in large half-marathon pacing and performance datasets, but direct training-response and prediction studies remain male-heavy. Observed age or sex differences do not establish subgroup-specific automatic training doses.

- **Sources:** `leyk-2007`, `nikolaidis-2019-ljubljana`, `cuk-2019-vienna`, `gomez-molina-2017`, `alvero-cruz-2019`, `nikolaidis-knechtle-2023-hm-predictors`
- **Population:** Adult women and men across age groups in observational race datasets
- **Domain:** Subgroup applicability; Age context; Sex context
- **Limitations:**
  - Observational pacing and participation data do not establish dose response
  - Direct predictor studies are predominantly male
  - Unknown sex or age cannot be converted into a hidden default
- **Verified effect estimates:**
  - Observed performance decline from age 50 to 69: 2.6 to 4.4 percent_per_decade (Large marathon and half-marathon observational dataset in Leyk et al. 2007)

## Known gaps

- A prospectively evaluated deterministic adult road half-marathon plan-generation policy
- A randomized adult road half-marathon longest-run dose or share trial
- Direct comparison of one versus two quality sessions per week in half-marathon planning
- A validated exact hard-session spacing rule for half-marathon training
- A validated baseline freshness window or minimum recent-history threshold
- A validated execution-window length, reassessment cadence, or aggressive short-horizon alternative
- Exact half-marathon threshold, race-specific, long-run, recovery, and fueling-practice templates
- Direct female-specific and masters-specific half-marathon training-response evidence
- A validated activity-average-power intensity prescription

## Conflicting findings

- Different intensity distributions improve endurance performance without one universal winner.
- Post-half-marathon recovery varies by outcome and post-race behavior rather than following one universal one-to-three-day rule.
- Carb-loading guidance changes around expected duration, so the half-marathon distance label alone is insufficient.
- Higher volume and longer runs associate with performance, while observational evidence cannot establish causal dose or safety.

## Follow-up questions

- Which direct-result freshness boundary should qualify current half-marathon capability?
- How much stable recent history is enough to anchor volume, frequency, quality density, and longest-run exposure?
- Should v1 select any exact quality-session ceiling, low-intensity floor, or long-run cap?
- Which execution window and reassessment cadence should be reviewed before activation?
- Should an athlete without a recent direct half-marathon result remain readiness-only rather than receive a maximal benchmark?
- How should expected duration and prior gastrointestinal tolerance change fueling prompts?
- Which taper choice should be selected from the indirect 8-to-14-day and 41-to-60-percent evidence?
- How should aggressive targets or short horizons route without catch-up or target-gap dose escalation?
- Which post-plan outcomes are sufficiently comparable for descriptive evaluation?
- Do eligibility, edits, fueling tolerance, recovery, and outcomes differ across sex, age, provider, and missingness groups?
